Know what each layer means

Three sources. Three different jobs.

National Weather Service

Wind and precipitation are a movement setup signal. They do not prove birds flew over a region.

eBird

Recent observations show what people have actually reported on the ground. Reporting effort varies by place and day.

BirdCast

Use the official Michigan dashboard to confirm radar-detected nocturnal migration and migration forecasts. This site does not scrape BirdCast.

One product, two peak seasons

Michigan spring and fall migration

The same page changes with the migration season, so this URL can build authority year after year instead of splitting spring and fall into competing pages.

Spring: March 1–June 15

Northbound migration builds from waterfowl and blackbirds into sparrows, shorebirds, warblers, vireos, thrushes, and flycatchers. Southerly winds are often the supportive directional signal.

Fall: August 1–November 15

Southbound migration progresses from shorebirds and early songbirds into warblers, vireos, thrushes, raptors, sparrows, kinglets, cranes, and waterfowl. Northerly winds are often the supportive directional signal.

Method

How Michigan Migration Today makes a recommendation

The regional outlook combines National Weather Service forecasts with the count and recency of eBird species reports in each focal county. Favorable directional wind can strengthen a movement setup; rain may create a grounding watch; recent observations strengthen the case that a region is worth checking. Those are planning signals only. Radar-detected migration is verified on BirdCast, and local presence is verified through eBird and this site's county/species pages.
