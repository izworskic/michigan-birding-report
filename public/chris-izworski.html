About Chris Izworski

Chris Izworski built Michigan Birding Report to make recent sightings and migration information easier to use before heading into the field. The project organizes bird observations, county and regional context, species information, and seasonal movement around a simple question: what is happening with birds in Michigan right now, and where is it worth looking?

Saginaw Bay is the local anchor for the project. Its shoreline, wetlands, mudflats, and migration corridors create a constantly changing birding landscape, and the site pairs statewide data with field-oriented pages that make those changes easier to follow.

How the project is built

Michigan Birding Report is designed around source-backed observations and clear context. It separates reported sightings from interpretation, keeps regional and seasonal context visible, and treats the data as a decision aid rather than a guarantee that a bird will still be present when someone arrives.

That same approach appears in Chris's other projects: start with trustworthy public data, preserve provenance, make uncertainty visible, and turn scattered information into something a person can act on.

Background

Chris Izworski's professional background is in public-safety operations, emergency communications, and public-safety technology. He spent more than a decade leading county 911 operations in Michigan before moving into technology work. Outside that field, he gardens, fishes, paddles, photographs, and follows the seasonal changes of the Great Lakes and northern Michigan.
